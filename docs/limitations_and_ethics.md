# Limitations and ethical considerations

## What this analysis can and cannot say

**Access is not the same as need.** Children in deprived areas are seen at higher
rates (age-standardised rate ratio 1.34 for the most against the least deprived
quintile). Their mental-health needs are also higher. Higher access in deprived
areas does **not** show that provision is fair relative to need. That needs a valid
small-area estimate of prevalence, which no current public dataset provides at
2026 Sub-ICB level.

**Lower recorded access by ethnic group may have several causes.** It may reflect
genuine barriers (awareness, stigma, culturally appropriate provision, referral
routes). It may reflect different underlying prevalence. It may also be partly
recording artefacts: 13% of MHS95 records have missing or invalid ethnicity, and
NHS England's population denominators come from the 2021 Census. The "Other ethnic
groups" rate (1.32× White) is especially sensitive to how records and denominators
are coded. These are the published NHS England figures; this project does not
re-derive them.

**Ecological fallacy.** Every association here is between *areas*, not children.
"More deprived areas have higher access" does not mean "deprived children are more
likely to be seen".

**Data completeness and recording.** MHSDS covers NHS-funded services that submit
to the dataset. Some school-based and voluntary-sector support is under-recorded or
missing. Differences between areas can come from recording practice as well as from
care. The over-dispersion factor (φ ≈ 281) reflects all of these sources combined.

**Snapshot.** Waits are a 3-month window (Jun–Aug 2026) and an end-of-August
waiting-list snapshot. Access is a 12-month rolling count. These figures are
Performance data and may be revised.

**Estimated denominators.** The Sub-ICB under-18 population is estimated (see the
methodology). ICB-level crude rates published by NHS England are the authoritative
figures.

**Waiting-time measures differ.** Median days to first contact for children *who
were seen* (MHS131, median about 17 days across areas) is very different from days
waited so far by children *still waiting* (MHS134, median about 245 days). The first
leaves out the people who are still waiting. Quoting it alone would understate the
problem, so both are reported.

## Ethical considerations

- **No personal data.** Only aggregate open statistics are used. Disclosure-control
  suppression (`*`) is kept and never back-calculated.
- **Not a league table.** Funnel plots are used so that areas aren't ranked on
  noise. "Outside the limits" is a prompt to ask questions, not a performance
  judgement.
- **Language.** Terminology follows NHS England's "children and young people (CYP)"
  wording, which avoids stigmatising labels.
- **Independence.** This is a personal portfolio project. It is not NHS-endorsed
  analysis, and it should not be used for commissioning decisions without the
  quality assurance an official analysis would get.
